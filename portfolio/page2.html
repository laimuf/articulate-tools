Peer volunteers supporting young people aged 18–25 across the UK needed safeguarding field guides that balanced organisational branding with clear, accurate safety information. As the organisation was moving to a digital-first approach, the guides needed to be designed for digital use from the outset. They also needed to feel modern and engaging for the age group, rather than having the traditional, formal feel of typical safeguarding materials.

I attended training sessions and reviewed materials to understand how peer volunteers engage with safeguarding content. I applied andragogy principles to design the handbooks. Since volunteers are adults with prior training experience, I focused on letting them identify what they need to know, building on their existing knowledge, and designing content around real-world scenarios they'd encounter in the field.

Working with subject matter experts, we determined two distinct handbooks were needed: one for team leaders and one for volunteer members. The team leader version balances text with minimal imagery, focusing on guidance and protocols. The volunteer version uses illustrations and visuals throughout for quick reference, making content feel more approachable and relatable.

Two handbooks were produced in Canva - one for volunteer leaders and one for members. Canva was ideal for this context: volunteers were already familiar with the platform through creating zines, making the interface intuitive. It also enabled nationwide use, whilst keeping safeguarding information accessible and shareable rather than formal and distant.
