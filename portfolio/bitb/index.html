The Problem

Browser-in-the-Browser (BITB) phishing attacks exploit familiar single sign-on workflows. Staff were entering credentials out of habit, trusting pop-ups that looked like systems they recognised, especially when messages felt urgent. The issue wasn’t lack of awareness. Staff knew phishing existed. The real problem was that they didn’t pause to check whether a login request was genuine before entering their credentials.

Goal: Enable employees (especially non-technical staff) to recognise and respond to BITB attacks with confidence.

The Design Approach

The aim wasn’t to explain BITB attacks in detail, but to change behaviour when faced with an urgent email or login window, pause, check, and report.

I focused on realistic practice and made the hidden parts of the attack visible. Explanations were kept short and visual to reduce cognitive load and support quick decisions. Each activity showed clear cause and effect:

  • A phishing email simulation reflected real inbox conditions and time pressure
  • A “BITB monster” visualised the attacker behind a familiar login window, prompting staff to stop and check
  • Short videos explained only what was needed to support action, using simple visuals rather than technical language

The Solution

Short targeted visual and practical learning experience focused on three key areas:

  • Recognising BITB warning signs

    An HTML-based inbox simulation and short animated story built relevance and prompted reflection on warning signs.

  • Understanding how BITB attacks work

    A short video showed how BITB exploits SSO workflows and introduced the three key steps of the attack.

  • Applying the correct response

    A video modelled the correct behaviour (spot–stop–report), followed by a sorting exercise to practise in context.

The course was built in Articulate Rise, with Vyond animations and light HTML customisation. Staff can safely observe mistakes, see realistic consequences, and practise the correct response before facing real attacks.
